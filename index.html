About

First the apps and your data.
Then the AI.

nenva starts as a canvas for apps. A task manager, a notes app, a journal, an email client, a calendar, an investment portfolio. Apps you already know, living together inside one desktop application on your Mac.

Your Mac
Tasks Notes Email Calendar Investment Portfolio

What you create is saved to your Mac's SSD

When you create a task or write a note, it lands on the disk inside your Mac, not on someone's server. Your data stays private on your machine, and you can be confident of that.

Optionally, turn on backups so you never lose anything: a copy of your apps' data, saved to a folder in your own iCloud Drive, private storage that only your machines can reach. If you ever need to restore to a point in time, you can do that easily.

Your apps → Your Mac's SSD ⇢ iCloud Drive backup · optional

That's the privacy promise, implemented. No one else can access your data. Not even the nenva team. There is nothing on our side to breach, to subpoena, or to sell.

A claim you can test: unplug the network, and the apps, your data, and a local AI model all keep working.

Now, let's introduce AI: what if you didn't have to keep watch yourself?

Up to this point, you drive the apps yourself: plan a project, break it into tasks with dates, and work through them with clarity. That alone is a good, organized life.

But instead of you keeping watch on what needs doing today, what if a personal AI tracked your schedule and slotted each new thing into the right place?

A meeting

On your calendar, shown in today's list before you ask.

An action item buried in an email

Found by AI analysis and filed into Actions on the right day.

A renewal you cannot miss

Spotted in the inbox and scheduled ahead of the deadline.

A subscription you didn't know you were still paying

Surfaced from billing email so you can decide if it stays.

An assistant like that has to understand plain language, the kind that lives in your emails, calendar events, and documents. And hence the need for AI.

What an LLM is: a model of language itself

AI is quite different from the technologies before it: apps, web services, databases. Its current engine is the Large Language Model, and the name is literal: a model of language information. English has around 150,000 words and countless ways to arrange them into meaning.

Think of an LLM as the computer version of a genius student who has read all the English text on the internet. Hand it an email or a document, and it can tell you what information is in there.

Some of the best models are free

LLMs are created through an expensive process called training, by companies that spend millions of dollars on it. Some keep their models behind closed doors, reachable only through their cloud. Others release theirs for the public to use directly, without paying the company that built them.

Open-weight models

Models anyone can download and run on their own computer, and use to understand any piece of text or image, on their own machine.

nenva brings the three together

nenva identifies which models are right for your Mac, then puts the apps, your data, and the AI in one place, so you can plan a project, keep track of your daily schedule, research whatever you need to do, and create the documents your work and personal life call for, with the core promise intact: your data stays private.

On a lighter Mac, you can instead bring your own OpenAI or Anthropic key. Always your explicit choice, never a default.

And because the assistant can do real things, its manners are built in: it asks before a routine is armed to run on its own, and before anything destructive happens.
