Welcome

Meet Anjadhe.

Anjadhe is a privacy-first personal assistant. It starts out as a blank canvas — just you and an assistant you can talk to. Ask it to:

  • Plan a project — break it into tasks and fit them into your schedule.
  • Keep up with your email — connect your inbox and it catches the renewals, receipts, and action items hiding in your mail.
  • Shape an investment strategy — around your goals and risk comfort, with a nudge when your holdings drift from the plan.
  • …and more. We’re just getting started.

Everything it does lands in simple, familiar apps — tasks, calendar, notes, portfolio — for the two of you to manage together. And it’s private first: your data lives on this Mac, with no account to create and nothing uploaded to us.

A large language model is your assistant’s brain, and running one well on the Mac itself takes 32 GB of memory or more — more than this Mac has. For a Mac this size, Anjadhe suggests Anjadhe Cloud:

Anjadhe Cloud An open-weight model hosted by Anjadhe — free monthly allowance, no account or key. What you send is never stored or used to train models.

Let your assistant search the web?

For current events, prices, and anything beyond what the model knows, the assistant can search the web through Anjadhe Connect — built in, no account, 300 searches a month included. When it searches, the query — and only the query — leaves this Mac; the service keeps a usage count, not what you searched, and its source code is public so that can be checked. Your emails, notes, and files never ride along.

Turn it on or off anytime in Settings › AI Assistant › Web Search — or use your own Tavily or Brave key there instead.

Where should your data live?

In a private folder on this Mac — and it stays there. Your tasks, goals, notes, journal, and portfolio all live here; Anjadhe has no remote database holding a copy, which is part of why the core app is free. Your data isn’t our product — we don’t collect it or sell it, and the app’s source code is public, so none of this is taken on faith.

Nothing syncs or uploads unless you turn it on. Use more than one Mac? Anjadhe can keep them all up to date through an encrypted change journal in your own iCloud Drive — no account beyond the iCloud you already have.

Change any of this anytime in Settings › Storage & Backup — a custom storage folder lives there too.

Give your assistant a name?

Optional, but it makes this yours. The name shows up across the app, and your assistant knows it and answers to it. You can change or remove it anytime in Settings › AI Assistant.

    About

    First the apps and your data.
    Then the AI.

    Anjadhe starts as a canvas for apps. A task manager, a notes app, a journal, an email client, a calendar, an investment portfolio. Apps you already know, living together inside one desktop application on your Mac.

    Your Mac
    Tasks Notes Journal Email Calendar Investment Portfolio

    What you create is saved to your Mac's SSD

    When you create a task or write a note, it lands on the disk inside your Mac, not on someone's server. Your data stays private on your machine, and you can be confident of that.

    Optionally, turn on backups so you never lose anything: a copy of your apps' data, saved to a folder in your own iCloud Drive, private storage that only your machines can reach. If you ever need to restore to a point in time, you can do that easily.

    Your apps → Your Mac's SSD ⇢ iCloud Drive backup · optional

    That's the privacy promise, implemented. No one else can access your data. Not even the Anjadhe team. There is nothing on our side to breach, to subpoena, or to sell.

    A claim you can test: unplug the network, and the apps, your data, and a local AI model all keep working.

    Now, let's introduce AI: what if you didn't have to keep watch yourself?

    Up to this point, you drive the apps yourself: plan a project, break it down into goals, split those into tasks, and work through them with clarity. That alone is a good, organized life.

    But instead of you keeping watch on what needs doing today, what if a personal assistant tracked your schedule and slotted each new thing into the right place?

    An assistant like that has to understand plain language, the kind that lives in your emails, calendar events, and documents. And hence the need for AI.

    What an LLM is: a model of language itself

    AI is quite different from the technologies before it: apps, web services, databases. Its current engine is the Large Language Model, and the name is literal: a model of language information. English has around 150,000 words and countless ways to arrange them into meaning.

    Think of an LLM as the computer version of a genius student who has read all the English text on the internet. Hand it an email or a document, and it can tell you what information is in there.

    Some of the best models are free

    LLMs are created through an expensive process called training, by companies that spend millions of dollars on it. Some keep their models behind closed doors, reachable only through their cloud. Others release theirs for the public to use directly, without paying the company that built them.

    Open-weight models

    Models anyone can download and run on their own computer, and use to understand any piece of text or image, on their own machine.

    Anjadhe brings the three together

    Anjadhe identifies which models are right for your Mac, then puts the apps, your data, and the AI in one place, so you can plan a project, keep track of your daily schedule, research whatever you need to do, and create the documents your work and personal life call for, with the core promise intact: your data stays private.

    On a lighter Mac, you can instead bring your own OpenAI or Anthropic key. Always your explicit choice, never a default.

    And because the assistant can do real things, its manners are built in: it asks before a routine is armed to run on its own, and before anything destructive happens.
