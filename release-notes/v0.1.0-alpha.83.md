# v0.1.0-alpha.83

## Added

- **Simple home is how Anjadhe opens now.** The window centres on one place
  to type, with **Needs you** under it (the things your AI genuinely wants a
  decision on) and **Ongoing work** folded beneath that. The cards you knew
  are still there, a click away under **Widgets**. Nothing about your data,
  your permissions or which model answers has changed — this is the same app
  wearing a quieter front. Settings › Appearance › **Simple home** turns it
  off and brings the old home back.
- **What your routines said, on the front page.** Under the composer, one
  row per routine that has something unread: its name, its headline and when
  it ran. A routine that stopped to ask you something appears in **Needs
  you** instead, because that is a question, not a result.
- **Log journal when it is due.** The shortcuts row leads with a nudge when
  you have not written in a while, and stands down once you have.

## Changed

- **The Web Browser app is gone, and links open in your own browser.**
  Anjadhe no longer carries a browser of its own. Every link you follow — in
  chat, notes, journal, a routine's result, News, Portfolio, Bookmarks —
  opens in the browser you already use, where you are already signed in.
  Reading a web page for you is unchanged: the assistant still fetches and
  summarises pages on your Mac, and the News reader is untouched. Settings ›
  Browser (the built-in browser's search engine) and Privacy › Clear browse
  data have gone with it.
- **Bookmarks is its own app.** It used to live behind the Web Browser's new
  tab page. It now has its own place in the sidebar and its own switch on
  the More apps page; your saved links, tags and groups are exactly as they
  were, and clicking one opens it in your browser.
- **Inbox no longer labels threads "Needs reply" or "Waiting on them".** It
  did not work well enough to keep, so the two sidebar rows and the labels
  are gone. Everything else about Inbox and Insights is unchanged.
- **Inbox bundles start off.** Turn them on in the Inbox's own settings if
  you want them.
- **Syncing with your phone away from home is much faster.** The Mac now
  dials the relay alongside a direct connection instead of after it, so a
  sync from another network stops paying the full wait for the direct
  attempt first. At home the direct connection still wins, and nothing goes
  through the relay.
- **Opening the app is quicker when another Mac has synced.** Anjadhe now
  reads another Mac's sync file only when it has actually changed, instead
  of re-reading and re-comparing all of them on every launch. On a large
  library that is the difference between a noticeable pause and none.
- **A first-run wizard that reads better.** The welcome step is one hero and
  three short cards instead of a feature list, the storage step states the
  facts plainly and treats the backup checkbox as a decision, and the name
  field is back on the type scale. No promise about your data changed — only
  how plainly it is put — and the copy no longer names an inference provider
  it has no business naming.
- **"Chat History" in the titlebar** rather than "History", which read as
  browsing history.
- **Repeat this gets a wider sheet** and shows a spinner while it rewrites
  your question as a standing one.
- **The AI Activity icon is back in the titlebar** on the simple shell.
- **Suggested prompts are the built-in set again.** The AI-written ones are
  behind an experimental switch, off by default; they were not earning their
  hourly call.

## Fixed

- **Logging a journal entry no longer asks for the app lock** when the
  classic home never did.
- **Portfolio's headline value and the setup name field** were set larger
  than the type scale allows.
- **The Inbox bundle sweep bar had no side padding.**
- **What's new could not open.** A stray apostrophe in one release's notes
  stopped the whole What's new list from loading. It never reached a
  released build.

## For the curious

- The workroom preview (Settings › Developer › Experimental features) now
  drives Google Chrome only; its built-in browser went with the Web Browser
  app. It remains off by default and unfinished.
