# v0.1.0-alpha.27

A short release, and all of it is about the promise the app makes rather than
what it can do. Preparing the Google OAuth verification material meant reading
our own claims against our own code, which turned up three places where the two
had drifted: a launch quietly called Google for fonts, every backup put the
whole database into iCloud unencrypted for a moment before encrypting it, and
two model downloads shipped without a checksum to verify them against.

## Fixed

- **Backups never put the plaintext database in iCloud, even for a moment.**
  The backup ran `VACUUM INTO` straight at the iCloud folder and encrypted the
  file afterwards, in place — so on every backup, hourly by default, the whole
  unencrypted database sat in the synced folder for the length of the encrypt.
  Whether iCloud uploaded that version before it was overwritten was a matter
  of timing nobody controls. Worse, the file stayed in cleartext if anything
  failed in that window, which the fail-closed encryption path made a real
  possibility rather than a theoretical one. Backups now build in a staging
  folder outside iCloud, are encrypted there, and only the finished ciphertext
  moves across; a backup that cannot be encrypted is not written at all.
- **The two most common models are now checksum-verified.** `gemma4:12b-it-qat`
  and `gemma4:e2b-it-qat` shipped with an empty hash pin, so their downloads
  were accepted without being checked — the two models a new install is most
  likely to pick were the two going unverified. All nine models with weights
  are now pinned.

## Changed

- **Starting the app no longer contacts Google for fonts.** The interface
  typefaces were fetched from Google's font service on every launch, which
  meant an unconditional request to Google, carrying your IP address, before
  you had connected any account at all. The fonts now ship inside the app. The
  same request was being made again on every user-built app you opened.
- **The privacy policy now names every service the app can talk to**, what it
  sends there, and why — replacing a claim that only three destinations existed,
  which was not true. It also states plainly that your data is never sold, and
  it now lists the exact OAuth scopes the app requests rather than an
  approximation of them.
